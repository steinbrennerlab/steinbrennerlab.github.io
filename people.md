<h3>Current grads, postdocs, and staff</h3>

<strong>
Ekkachai Khwanbua <br>
Postdoctoral Researcher <br>
</strong>
Ekkachai was born and raised in Thailand and later moved to the States to pursue his PhD in Plant Pathology at Iowa State University, which he completed in December 2025. He joined the Steinbrenner lab in May 2026 as a postdoc. He is excited to learn more about plant receptor biology, including receptor-like proteins (RLPs). Outside the lab, he enjoys hiking, swimming, cooking, and reading books.

<img src="/images/people/moore.jpg" class="align-left" alt="" width="300">
<strong>
Amy Moore <br>
Graduate Student <br>
amoore02 /at/ uw.edu <br>
</strong>
I am a Biology graduate student who joined the lab in 2025. I previously worked as an undergraduate at the University of Minnesota in the lab of Fumi Katagiri studying the robust plant immune signaling network and as a post-bac at the University of Washington in the Brewer/Raghuraman lab using the power of yeast genetics to study DNA replication timing and origin usage. In the Steinbrenner lab I hope to understand immune recognition specificity in legumes, especially when it comes to responding to our favorite herbivore pests.

<img src="/images/people/sheppard.jpg" class="align-left" alt="" width="300">
<strong>
Ben Sheppard <br> 
Graduate Student <br>
bdshep /at/ uw.edu <br>
</strong>
I am graduate student studying molecular mechanisms of immune signaling in plants. Previously I studied plant genetics as an undergraduate at Whitman College, and worked in human vaccine research at Fred Hutchinson Cancer Center. My key interest is in understanding how the molecular perception of pathogens and pests induces threat-specific immune responses in plants. My current work focuses on the role of signaling kinases following plant perception of caterpillar herbivory by the legume Inception Receptor.
<BR CLEAR="left">

Adam Steinbrenner <br>

</strong>
Associate Professor, UW Dept. of Biology<br>
HHMI Postdoctoral Fellow, UC San Diego (2016-2019)<br>
PhD 2015, UC Berkeley Dept. of Plant & Microbial Biology<br>
BS 2010, Tufts University<br><br>
I am fascinated by plant immune systems. Building on my training in chemical ecology and molecular genetics of immune receptors, my lab now explores new aspects of plant immunity, often using comparative, evolutionary, and synthetic biology lenses. I love working with excited, creative trainees to develop projects on these topics.
